i

How it works

Each weekend is simulated thousands of times. Pace is measured as % off the fastest car: qualifying from every session's lap times, race pace from each driver's median clean race lap, recent races counting more, mixed with the team-mate's. Practice nudges this weekend's pace and, for the next race, the betting market (Kalshi win / podium / top-10 / pole odds) pulls it towards what the market expects (Market weight).

Every simulated weekend draws a form swing per team and per driver that carries through qualifying, sprint and race, redraws each driver's pace and each team's reliability within how sure the model is about them, and rolls for rain (forecast when in range), multi-car crashes and a safety car (likelier after a crash; it shuffles the order). The finishing order is race pace plus a cost for each grid slot, bigger where passing is hard. Overtakes depend on the grid slot and how much a driver moves through the field; pit-stop points come from each team's real stop times.

Circuits start from past seasons there (2014 on: overtaking, retirements, how much the grid decides; 2023 on: safety cars, rain) and are scaled by this season's trend against those same circuits, so a new rule set that brings more overtakes shows up after a few rounds. The trend restarts every season. Every setting is tested walk-forward on this season (only what was known before each round).

Every weekend is scored with the official 2026 rules: qualifying 10–1, race 25–1, sprint 8–1, ±1 per place (sprint losses capped at −10), −20 race / −10 sprint for not classifying, the constructor Q2/Q3 bonus and pit-stop bands.

  • Transfers: 2 free per race; 1 unused carries over. Extras cost −10.
  • Chips: WC and LL remove the transfer limit (LL also the budget). X3 triples your top driver and doubles the second. NN counts every negative scoring event as 0. AP gives the Boost to your highest scorer.

Circuits
i
Each circuit starts from its past seasons (overtaking, retirements, how much the grid decides, safety cars, rain), scaled by how this season compares with past seasons at the circuits raced so far. A new circuit borrows from tracks of the same type (Power, Street, Fast corners). Overtaking is relative to this season's average, Grid decides is the expected grid-to-finish rank correlation. Move a slider to override a race; the reset button in Model settings restores the fitted values.
